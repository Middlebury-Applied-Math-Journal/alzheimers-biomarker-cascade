# Krysta Pereira1

My first memory of math occurred in first grade when everyone flipped their behavior sticks from green to yellow because a substitute teacher said we were all in trouble. My stick had never turned yellow in my first two years of elementary school, so I was devastated. That changed when the substitute brought out addition and subtraction flashcards and made a deal: beat your partner, and you could turn your stick back to green. I perked up and, by the end, my stick turned green. In that moment, I felt confident and excited by the challenge. It was the first time I remember feeling exhilarated by math. From then on, I looked for ways to stretch my brain in math. When I first took Euclidean and Non-Euclidean Geometries at Middlebury, I initially felt confident but soon fell into imposter syndrome. At home, I had been used to advocating for myself, but here everything felt di!erent. I didn’t know how to navigate asking for help or approach peers. I felt overwhelmed and as though my love for math was fading. After that semester, I began to rediscover my love for math in a new way, by finding a community of people who shared my joy in mathematics. My peers’ encouragement helped me reconnect with my passion and motivated me to continue pursuing math after graduation, transforming moments where I almost gave up into renewed joy and love for the subject. Without the support of my mentors, peers, and family, I would have likely stopped pursuing mathematics much earlier. I have learned the importance of community, passion, and perseverance, and I am excited to continue pursuing math and finding communities like the one I found at Middlebury.
