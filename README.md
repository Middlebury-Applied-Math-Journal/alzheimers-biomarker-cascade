# Investigating the Biomarker Cascade of Alzheimer’s Disease

Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive, irreversible brain disorder that gradually destroys cognitive function and memory, mak- ing even the simplest tasks di!cult to perform. While the general pathological mechanisms underlying Alzheimer’s dis- ease are known, the timing and progression of these changes in the brain remain unclear. Previous models suggest that a set of biomarkers evolves over time in a characteristic se- quence associated with worsening disease. This framework, known as the biomarker cascade theory, proposes that beta- amyloid and tau aggregation initiate the pathological process, followed by cognitive decline and ultimately death. While experiments have supported this claim, they are limited in their ability to understand the mechanisms underlying the biomarker cascade. Thus, we turn to math modeling to bet- ter understand the timing dynamics and interactions of the biomarkers in this cascade. In this study, we analyze a dy- namical systems framework to better understand how inter- actions among biomarkers influence disease progression. By examining the system parameters, we aim to identify which biomarkers exert the greatest influence on the cascade and to explore potential early indicators of Alzheimer’s disease, ultimately informing improved treatment strategies.
